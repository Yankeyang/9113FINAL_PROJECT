# Research Question

## Main Question

Where is near-home public EV charging demand concentrated in California, and which census tracts appear underserved because modeled demand is high while existing public charger supply is low?

## Subquestions

1. Where are the strongest clusters of CEC-modeled near-home public charging demand?
2. How do existing public Level 2 and DC fast chargers overlap with those demand areas?
3. Which Los Angeles and San Francisco census tracts have the largest demand-supply gaps?
4. Are underserved areas associated with income, housing type, renter share, vehicle availability, commuting patterns, or disadvantaged community status?
5. Can a model trained on Los Angeles census tracts explain or predict similar demand-supply gaps in San Francisco?

## Working Hypothesis

Near-home public charging gaps are expected to be higher in dense urban areas with more multifamily housing, more renters, and less access to home charging. Some low-income or disadvantaged communities may show high need even when existing charger counts are limited.
